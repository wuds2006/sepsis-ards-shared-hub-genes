# Supplementary Methods

With the Path-A restructure of 2026-10-16, the complete Methods moved from the main text into this supplement; the main-text METHODS is now a summary that points here (Supplementary Methods S1–S14). Sections expanded at that merge: S2 (selection stability, disease prediction models), S3 (ssGSEA), S6 (meta-analysis), S7 (power analysis, LDSC), S9 (GO/KEGG enrichment), and S12 (repurposing, docking, MM/GBSA, druggability). Citations within this supplement use S-numbered references (see Supplementary References at the end of this file).

Sections S1-S7 are numbered by first-citation order in the revised main text; sections S8-S13 provide additional detail in their original order (renumbering of 2026-09-25; see 05_Renumbering_Maps.md).

## S1. Data acquisition and sample curation

Raw series matrices and platform annotations were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO). Sample metadata (source names, titles, and group labels) were reconstructed directly from the original GEO records. For GSE76293 (ARDS), the series contained 12 ARDS patients, 12 healthy volunteers (HVTs), and 70 in vitro-cultured PMN samples treated with GM-CSF and/or PI3K inhibitors; the 70 cultured samples were excluded. For GSE69528 (sepsis), the series contained 83 sepsis patients, 28 uninfected healthy controls, and 27 uninfected patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus; the 27 diabetic samples were excluded. Exclusion was performed on the raw group label and verified against the pre-filtered group object by set equality. Sampling time relative to admission was recorded where annotated (GSE65682, ICU admission; GSE243066, within 24 hours of admission); for GSE76293, GSE69528, and GSE100159, the sampling time and donor ancestry are not annotated in the local series records [AUTHOR_INPUT_NEEDED: confirm from GEO records].

## S2. Machine-learning and SHAP

Eleven classifiers (LASSO, ridge, elastic net, random forest, support vector machine, k-nearest neighbors, naive Bayes, neural network, logistic regression, XGBoost, and decision tree) were trained on the 179 shared candidate genes separately in each discovery cohort (GSE76293 as the ARDS training set and GSE69528 as the sepsis training set) with five-fold cross-validation. Model discrimination was summarized by AUC. SHAP values were computed on a random forest refit on the union of LASSO-selected and random-forest top-30 genes and quantified the contribution of each gene to model output within each training set. Candidate hub genes were defined operationally as genes selected by LASSO that also ranked in the top 30 by random-forest Gini importance or in the top 20 by mean |SHAP| value; when fewer than five genes met this rule, the top 15 by mean |SHAP| were carried forward. A gradient-boosting-machine classifier (gbm package) was additionally evaluated but was not carried into consensus ranking.

Selection stability was assessed by LASSO bootstrap selection frequencies (200 resamples), random-forest reruns across 50 seeds, and perturbation of the ranking thresholds (random-forest top 20 to top 40, SHAP top 10 to top 30) with Jaccard similarity of the resulting candidate sets; cross-validated AUCs were checked against label permutation (500 shuffles; permutation P≤0.014). The consensus rule yielded nine ARDS and six sepsis candidate hub genes.

Disease prediction models used the six hub genes as predictors in disease-specific logistic regression models constructed with the lrm function of the rms package, with expression values z-score standardized using the training-cohort mean and SD, and ridge regularization (penalty = 1, chosen a priori for numerical stability rather than tuned to validation performance) to stabilize coefficients under complete separation in the small ARDS training cohort: an ARDS model trained on GSE76293 (n=24) and validated on GSE243066 (n=49), and a sepsis model trained on GSE69528 (n=111) and validated on GSE100159 (n=45). Nomograms were drawn with the nomogram function in rms; discrimination was assessed by ROC analysis (pROC), calibration by bootstrap calibration curves, and clinical net benefit by decision-curve analysis. Refit models using all seven ARDS-nominated genes served as the seven-gene sensitivity analysis.

## S3. Immune-cell deconvolution

Immune-cell deconvolution was performed with the CIBERSORT algorithm (S1; linear nu-support-vector regression against the LM22 leukocyte signature matrix) implemented in R (e1071 for support-vector regression; preprocessCore for quantile normalization). Mixture matrices were supplied in linear space (microarray data were converted from log2 to linear scale; RNA-seq data as counts per million), with quantile normalization applied to microarray but not RNA-seq mixtures, and 1,000 permutations per sample for the deconvolution P value. Group differences in CIBERSORT fractions were tested by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. Because deconvolution of non-granulocyte lineages from purified polymorphonuclear-neutrophil (PMN) fractions (GSE76293) does not reflect tissue infiltration, estimates from the PMN cohort were retained in Supplementary Table S2 for transparency but were not used to support any conclusion.

As a complementary, deconvolution-independent estimate of immune composition, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA; S2) was performed in all four bulk cohorts using marker gene sets of 18 immune-cell types assembled from published lineage markers (full list in Supplementary Table S3); cell types with fewer than two detectable marker genes in a cohort (Th17 in GSE69528) were not scored in that cohort. For each sample, all genes were ranked by decreasing expression and the enrichment of each gene set was quantified from the cumulative distribution functions of genes inside and outside the set along the ranked list. Scores were compared between disease and control groups by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment, and associations between ssGSEA cell-type scores and hub-gene expression were assessed by Spearman correlation. Because ssGSEA scores quantify marker-program activity rather than cell counts, and Ficoll enrichment depletes granulocytes, neutrophil-program scores in the PBMC cohort (GSE243066) were interpreted as myeloid-program activation rather than neutrophil infiltration, and ssGSEA estimates from the PMN cohort are reported for transparency only.

## S4. Single-cell processing

GSE151263 PBMC data (13,996 cells; three patients with ARDS and four with sepsis) were processed with Scanpy: quality filtering, normalization, log transformation, highly-variable-gene selection, and PCA. Batch effects were corrected with Harmony, followed by Leiden clustering and UMAP embedding. Cell types were annotated by canonical marker expression. CellChat inferred ligand–receptor interactions and compared them between ARDS and sepsis. slingshot reconstructed the monocyte-to-macrophage trajectory; gene–trajectory associations were quantified by Spearman correlation.

GSE167363 CD14+ monocyte data (12,359 cells; two healthy controls and two patients with sepsis, one sampled at two time points; five samples in total) were processed with Seurat: quality filtering, normalization, variable-gene selection, scaling, PCA, Harmony integration across samples, graph-based clustering, and UMAP embedding. Subpopulations were annotated by cluster-level marker-module scoring (mean module score per cluster, argmax assignment), with dedicated marker modules for classical (CD14, LST1, FCN1), inflammatory (IL1B, CCL3, SAA1, TNF), intermediate (FCGR3A, CD14), non-classical (FCGR3A, MS4A7, CX3CR1), and interferon-stimulated gene-high monocytes (ISG15, IFIT1, MX1), monocyte-derived dendritic cells (FCER1A, CLEC10A), and plasmacytoid dendritic cells (LILRA4, GZMB, IRF7). CellChat compared ligand–receptor interactions between sepsis and healthy controls, and pathway-level information flow was compared between groups (log2 fold change of summed interaction strength). slingshot was applied to the monocyte subpopulations with classical monocytes as the root; hub-gene expression along pseudotime was tested by Spearman correlation with Benjamini–Hochberg correction.

AUCell gene-set activity was quantified in both cohorts. For each of the six hub genes, a co-expression gene set was defined as the gene itself plus the 50 genes most positively correlated with it (Spearman correlation) within the single-cell normalized expression matrix, restricted to genes expressed in more than 3% of cells and excluding ribosomal protein and mitochondrial genes, with correlations computed on up to 4,000 randomly sampled cells (seed 42); a composite HubSignature gene set comprised the six hub genes. Per-cell recovery scores were computed with AUCell on the rank-normalized expression matrix, and group differences within each cell type were tested by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with Benjamini–Hochberg correction (GSE167363: sepsis versus healthy controls, 42 gene-set-by-cell-type comparisons; GSE151263: ARDS versus sepsis, 49 comparisons; comparisons with fewer than 10 cells per group were skipped).

An independent trajectory reconstruction was performed with Monocle3 (version 1.3.1) in both cohorts: preprocessing with 50 principal components, UMAP dimensionality reduction, Leiden community detection, and graph learning with partition-aware root selection (one root node per graph partition; root cell type: monocytes in GSE151263 and classical monocytes in GSE167363). Pseudotime was computed from each partition root, gene-expression spatial autocorrelation was quantified by Moran's I with graph-based permutation testing (graph_test), and group differences in pseudotime were tested by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. Pseudotime scales are cohort-specific and were not compared quantitatively across cohorts.

Metabolic pathway activity was scored in both cohorts: seven MSigDB Hallmark metabolic gene sets (glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid metabolism, cholesterol homeostasis, heme metabolism, bile acid metabolism, and xenobiotic metabolism) were scored per cell with scanpy score_genes, mean module scores were summarized by cell type and disease group, group-level differences were tested by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests, and Spearman correlations between hub-gene expression and pathway scores were computed per cohort.

Donor-level sensitivity analyses addressed cell-level pseudoreplication. For every key cell-level contrast, per-donor medians (pseudotime, AUCell UPP1-program recovery scores in monocytes) or means (metabolic pathway scores) were aggregated and group differences were tested by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. For GSE167363, the two samples from the repeatedly sampled sepsis patient (S2_T0 and S2_T6) were collapsed to a single donor before aggregation, yielding four patients (two sepsis, two healthy controls). All donor-level values with group assignments are provided in Supplementary Table S12 and visualized in Supplementary Figure S2. Cell-level P values are reported in the main text with the explicit caveat that they treat cells as independent units and overstate statistical precision; donor-level statistics are directionally consistent with every key cell-level contrast but underpowered at two to four donors per group.

## S5. Virtual knockout

scTenifoldKnk performed direct virtual knockout by tensor-decomposing the single-cell gene-regulatory network and simulating the transcriptome-wide response to gene deletion. In PBMCs, *UPP1* and *EXOSC4* were the only hub genes with sufficient expression and were knocked out. In CD14+ monocytes, four hub genes met the expression threshold after quality-control filtering (*UPP1*, 42.7% of cells; *CDK5RAP2*, 5.8%; *EXOSC4*, 3.0%; *VNN1*, 1.1%); for the two low-abundance genes the minimum expressed-cell fraction was relaxed (*EXOSC4* to 0.01 and *VNN1* to 0.005) to retain them in the filtered network. *CD177* (0.4% of cells) and *GRB10* (0.6%) fell below any workable threshold and were recorded as not amenable to knockout. Differentially regulated genes were identified by the scTenifoldKnk distance statistic with Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P values.

CellOracle built a base gene regulatory network (hg38 motif database) and cluster-specific networks by gradient-boosting regression, then simulated in silico knockout of the top-ranked upstream transcription factors to quantify their effect on hub-gene expression. In PBMCs, CEBPB, FOSL2, ETS2, YY1, MAX, and KLF2 were perturbed. In CD14+ monocytes, the ten top-ranked upstream transcription factors for the four knockable hub genes (including CEBPB, ENO1, KLF9, FOS, ID2, REL, STAT1, RAD21, NR3C1, and ETS2) were perturbed, and the change in simulated hub-gene expression (delta=simulated minus observed mean) was summarized as a transcription-factor-by-gene knockout-effect matrix.

For group-stratified analyses, both tools were additionally run within each disease group separately (ARDS and sepsis in GSE151263; sepsis and healthy controls in GSE167363). Group-stratified scTenifoldKnk used nc_nNet = 2 subnetworks of 500 cells each with the minimum expressed-cell fraction set to 0.005; genes expressed in fewer than 0.5% of cells within a group were recorded as not amenable to knockout in that group. Concordance of knockout fold-change profiles between groups was quantified by Spearman correlation over shared genes, with overlap of the top-150 affected genes summarized by the Jaccard index; the top-150 affected genes per group were functionally annotated by Gene Ontology biological-process enrichment (clusterProfiler, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P<0.05). Group-stratified CellOracle built transcriptional regulatory networks separately within each disease group and quantified CEBPB knockout effects on hub-gene expression per group.

## S6. External validation

Validation used GSE100159 (sepsis: 33 cases, 12 controls; GPL6884) and GSE243066 (ARDS: 34 cases, 15 controls; GPL30209; peripheral-blood mononuclear-cell RNA-seq on the MGISEQ-2000RS; blood collected within 24 hours of admission). Validation cohorts played no role in discovery-stage nomination: candidates were nominated by machine learning within discovery cohorts (S2) and screened cross-disease entirely within discovery data, whereby each candidate was tested as a single-gene classifier in the discovery cohort of the opposite disease and retained only with same-direction discrimination at AUC>0.7 (seven of nine ARDS-nominated and four of six sepsis-nominated candidates passed; DYRK2, TCTN1, and ZSCAN18 were direction-reversed and excluded; Supplementary Table S1). External validation then served as final-set confirmation: single-gene AUCs were computed directly from expression (no fitting), and six of the seven ARDS-nominated candidates were confirmed in both cohorts whereas MUC1 was refuted in GSE243066 (AUC=0.575); because this step reduced seven ARDS-nominated candidates to six, downstream validation estimates carry a mild optimistic bias bounded by the seven-gene sensitivity analysis. The primary multi-gene validation is the coefficient-transfer analysis of the discovery-trained logistic-regression models (validation AUC 0.984 for ARDS and 0.708 for sepsis; main text, Disease Prediction Model). An in-cohort refit of the combined signature in each validation cohort is reported descriptively only (AUC=1.000 in both; exact one-sided 95% lower confidence bounds 0.994 and 0.993, computed as alpha^(1/[n_case × n_control]) because the DeLong interval degenerates at perfect separation). As a disease-versus-disease contrast, patient-level pseudobulk expression of the six hub genes in the single-cell PBMC cohort (GSE151263; three ARDS versus four sepsis donors) was evaluated by ROC analysis with exact Mann-Whitney P values; none of the six genes discriminated ARDS from sepsis (per-gene AUC 0.33 to 0.75; combined signature AUC=0.50) (Supplementary Table S12). Hub status of the six genes was quantified post hoc by module membership (kME, computed from module eigengenes of the top disease-associated module in each discovery cohort) and co-expression degree centrality (|r| > 0.5 and |r| > 0.7) within the 179 shared candidate genes (Supplementary Table S10).

Per-gene effects across the two validation cohorts were combined by fixed- and random-effects meta-analysis of standardized mean differences (Hedges g), with heterogeneity quantified by I²; per-gene AUCs were reported with DeLong 95% confidence intervals and exact one-sided lower confidence bounds for AUC=1.000. Within-cohort standardized expression distributions were compared between training and validation cohorts by overlap coefficients and Cohen d. As an explicitly exploratory re-evaluation, a ComBat cross-platform batch adjustment (which pools discovery and validation data and therefore breaks the validation firewall) was applied to the six-gene models.

## S7. Mendelian randomization

Two-sample MR used the sepsis GWAS ieu-b-4980 (11,643 cases, 474,841 controls) and FinnGen R11 ARDS (endpoint J10_ARDS; 431 cases, 493,301 controls) bidirectionally. Because sepsis has few genome-wide-significant variants, instrumental variables were selected at P<5 × 10⁻⁶ and clumped (r² < 0.001, 10-Mb window; 1-Mb distance clumping for the reverse direction). Effect estimates were obtained by inverse-variance weighting, with MR-Egger, weighted median, and weighted mode as sensitivity analyses. Pleiotropy was assessed by the MR-Egger intercept, heterogeneity by Cochran's Q, and instrument strength by the F statistic. Directionality was confirmed by Steiger filtering. Cross-reference-genome matching used rsIDs rather than chromosome:position coordinates. Reverse MR (ARDS as exposure) retained seven instruments after 1-Mb clumping and harmonization (mean F statistic 22) and was likewise null (IVW P=0.329).

Post-hoc power analysis (Brion-type analytic calculation with simulation confirmation) quantified the minimum detectable causal odds ratio at 80% power for each direction (2.26 forward, 1.04 reverse). SNP heritability and genetic correlation between sepsis and ARDS were estimated by linkage-disequilibrium score regression (LDSC; S4). The plasma-protein accessibility of each hub gene was assessed from UniProt, the Human Protein Atlas, and the primary literature to determine whether protein-level Mendelian randomization was applicable; five of the six genes are intracellular or membrane-bound, leaving only soluble VNN1 amenable in principle.

SMR (v1.03) was performed with eQTLGen whole-blood cis-eQTL summary data (BESD format; 31,684 individuals) for the five hub genes with an available cis-eQTL instrument, against the sepsis (ieu-b-4980) and FinnGen R11 ARDS GWAS. The HEIDI test was run with local 1000 Genomes European linkage-disequilibrium references built with --make-bld (±2,000 kb windows). Bayesian colocalization (coloc) quantified the posterior probability of a shared causal variant (PP.H4) per gene and outcome. No gene showed a significant SMR association (all P_SMR > 0.1) or evidence of pleiotropy (all P_HEIDI > 0.05, range 0.287 to 0.855), and no gene-outcome pair showed colocalization (PP.H4 < 0.05 for all pairs).

## S8. Microarray preprocessing and probe-to-gene mapping

Affymetrix (GSE76293, GPL570) and Illumina (GSE69528, GPL10558) probe-level matrices were background-corrected and quantile-normalized using standard methods. Probe-to-gene mapping used the platform annotation files; for probes mapping to multiple genes, only the first gene symbol was retained. For genes represented by multiple probes, the probe with the highest mean expression across samples was selected as the representative.

## S9. Differential expression and WGCNA

Differential expression used limma with empirical Bayes moderation; genes with |log2 fold change| > 1 and Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P<0.05 were declared differentially expressed. WGCNA was performed on the 3,000 most variable genes using a signed network, with soft-thresholding powers selected by scale-free topology fit (power=10 for GSE76293; power=6 for GSE69528). Modules significantly correlated with disease status (P<0.05) were considered disease-associated.

Gene Ontology (biological process, molecular function, and cellular component) and KEGG pathway enrichment of the shared candidate genes and of the six hub genes were performed with the clusterProfiler R package (S3; enrichGO and enrichKEGG) using Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted P<0.05, and visualized as Sankey-bubble and chord diagrams. Cross-cohort module preservation was quantified by the modulePreservation statistic Zsummary (200 permutations) on the genes shared between the two discovery cohorts. Intramodular hub status was quantified post hoc by module membership (kME), computed from module eigengenes of the disease-associated modules, and by co-expression degree centrality (|r| > 0.5 and |r| > 0.7) within the 179 shared candidate genes.

## S10. GSVA of immune pathways

Immune-related GO biological-process gene sets were extracted from MSigDB c5.all.v7.5.1 by keyword matching (immune, inflammat, T/B cell, macrophage, cytokine, interferon, complement, toll-like, NF-kappaB, etc.) with explicit exclusion of false matches (cytokinesis, cilium, flagellum, neural crest). Gene sets containing 10–500 genes were retained (n=537). GSVA scores were computed per sample with a Gaussian kernel and tested by limma. The top 20 pathways by adjusted P value were visualized per cohort.

## S11. Protein-level validation

CD177 and VNN2 protein expression across diseases was queried from the HPA Blood Atlas (Olink proximity extension assay; 59 diseases, 6,074 individuals; accessed [AUTHOR_INPUT_NEEDED: Blood Atlas version and date]). Differential expression versus healthy controls was summarized as log2 fold change with false-discovery-rate-adjusted P values.

## S12. Drug repositioning and molecular docking

Drug repositioning used the six hub genes as the query signature, submitted through the Enrichr API to the Old CMap up-regulated and down-regulated gene-set libraries (S5), to DSigDB (S6), and to the Drug Perturbations from GEO library as an independent perturbation compendium; the top ten compounds per library, with nominal P values, are reported in Supplementary Table S18. Because any anti-inflammatory gene signature is expected to match glucocorticoid perturbation profiles, enrichment specificity was quantified with random gene-set nulls under three backgrounds: 100 random six-gene sets drawn from the 173 non-hub shared candidate genes, 100 drawn from all genes measured in both discovery cohorts (transcriptome-wide), and 100 drawn from the 123 genes up-regulated in both discovery cohorts with the hub genes excluded (a stringency-matched background that shares both the disease relevance and the coordinated up-regulation of the real signature), each submitted through the identical query protocol (same libraries, same API); for each null set we recorded whether any glucocorticoid term appeared in the top ten and the best glucocorticoid-term P value, and the real signature was compared against these null distributions (Supplementary Figure S18).

Molecular docking used AutoDock Vina 1.2.5 (S7) against ten natural-product ligands (EGCG, quercetin, luteolin, kaempferol, baicalein, resveratrol, curcumin, berberine, emodin, tetrahydroalstonine), prepared with Meeko. For UPP1 (PDB 3EUF, co-crystallized with the uridine analog BAU) and VNN1 (PDB 7SLV, co-crystallized with inhibitor 9TC), chain A, docking was site-directed at the co-crystallized ligand site (22 Å boxes centered on the ligand centroid); for CDK5RAP2, CD177, GRB10, and EXOSC4, for which no ligand-complexed experimental structure exists, AlphaFold-predicted structures were docked at 30 Å centroid boxes. Search depth was exhaustiveness 16 with five output poses per combination. The protocol was validated by redocking the co-crystallized ligands into their native sites: top-ranked poses reproduced the crystallographic poses with heavy-atom root-mean-square deviations of 1.54 Å (BAU, 3EUF) and 0.69 Å (9TC, 7SLV), both below the 2.0 Å criterion (per-mode values in Supplementary Figure S19). Per-residue model confidence (pLDDT) for the AlphaFold structures was summarized globally and within the docking box (Supplementary Table S19).

As an orthogonal affinity estimate, single-point MM/GBSA (molecular mechanics/generalized Born surface area) free energies were computed in OpenMM 8.5.1 for the five targets with high-confidence structures (50 combinations); CDK5RAP2 was excluded because its box-region model confidence was only moderate (mean pLDDT 73.5), and its docking energies are reported as exploratory only. The protein was described by the ff14SB force field with the GBn2 (OBC-II) implicit solvent model and no nonbonded cutoff; ligands were assigned Gasteiger partial charges and UFF Lennard-Jones parameters (Lennard-Jones terms of polar hydrogens zeroed, following the Amber convention), with generalized Born radii from the standard OBC parameter assignment. Because a bonded force field for the ligands was not available in this environment, each complex was first relaxed by local energy minimization with the ligand held by positional restraints (5,000 kJ/mol/nm²) and a short-range monotonic repulsive wall (15 kcal/mol × (1.5 Å/r)¹², ligand-involving pairs only) to prevent unphysical Coulombic collapse of oppositely charged atom pairs; the repulsive wall was excluded from the reported energies. Binding free energy was evaluated under a single-trajectory convention as ΔG = E(complex) − E(protein) − E(ligand) + γ·ΔSASA, with the nonpolar term estimated from Shrake–Rupley solvent-accessible surface areas (1.4 Å probe, γ = 0.005 kcal/mol/Å²). Protein-ligand interactions of the top-ranked complex (VNN1-luteolin) were enumerated with PLIP and rendered as a two-dimensional interaction diagram.

Target druggability and prior pharmacology were assessed systematically: tractability annotations (small-molecule, antibody, and other modality buckets) for each hub gene were retrieved from the Open Targets Platform (GraphQL API, September 2026 [AUTHOR_INPUT_NEEDED: confirm release number and exact date]), and known drug-target mechanisms were queried from ChEMBL (accessed [AUTHOR_INPUT_NEEDED: release and date]). A target was classified as having an "approved or clinical-stage" drug when ChEMBL recorded at least one small molecule with regulatory approval or an active Phase I–IV investigational indication against it; no hub gene met this criterion.

## S13. Single-gene gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA)

To functionally annotate each hub gene independently, single-gene GSEA was performed separately in all four bulk cohorts (GSE76293, ARDS neutrophils; GSE69528, sepsis whole blood; GSE243066, ARDS PBMC RNA-seq; GSE100159, sepsis whole blood). For each of the six hub genes (*CDK5RAP2*, *CD177*, *GRB10*, *UPP1*, *EXOSC4*, *VNN1*), a ranked list of all genes was generated by computing the Pearson correlation between the hub gene's expression and the expression of every other gene across samples; the hub gene itself was excluded, and correlations were ranked in descending order. Enrichment of the 50 MSigDB Hallmark gene sets (h.all.v2024.1.Hs.symbols, downloaded from the Broad Institute GSEA website) against each ranked list was assessed with the fgsea R package using the multilevel procedure, a minimum gene-set size of 10 and a maximum of 500, and the standard enrichment-score statistic. For each hub gene, the top two pathways ranked by absolute normalized enrichment score (NES) were retained and visualized as running-enrichment-score plots with the rank-metric (correlation) bar underneath. Because the ranked metric is a co-expression correlation rather than a disease-versus-control statistic, positive NES indicates that genes co-expressed with the hub gene are enriched in the pathway (a gene-centric functional annotation), and negative NES indicates enrichment among genes anti-correlated with the hub gene.

## S14. Prognostic modeling in GSE65682

Prognostic analysis was confined to GSE65682 (GPL13667, Affymetrix HG-U219), the only cohort among those analyzed with a survival endpoint. The processed series matrix was used as provided; probe-to-gene mapping used the platform annotation extracted from the family SOFT file, and probes were collapsed to gene symbols by the probe with the highest mean expression across samples. Of the 179 shared candidate genes, 171 were represented; four genes were not annotated on the platform (ZBTB42, PIK3R6, ZNF329, CCL23), and four additional mapped genes had no measurable expression and were dropped. The analysis set comprised the 479 patients with septic shock with a 28-day endpoint, after removing duplicate draws from the same patient (first sample retained). The endpoint was 28-day mortality (114 events, 23.8%); a recorded follow-up time of zero was recoded as 0.5 days. Time-dependent ROC used the inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting estimator (S8), with the 28-day evaluation placed at 27.99 days to avoid the administrative censoring boundary. Bootstrap optimism correction used 300 resamples; the LASSO penalty was held fixed at the full-cohort value within each resample, so the optimism estimate for the penalized models is, if anything, slightly conservative. For the net reclassification index and integrated discrimination improvement at 28 days (S9), events were deaths within 28 days (n=114), non-events were patients alive and still under follow-up at 28 days (n=363; the two patients whose last follow-up preceded 28 days without an event were excluded from this calculation), and the classification cutoff was the cohort event rate (0.239). The random survival forest used ranger (500 trees, minimum node size 15, mtry set to the square root of the number of predictors; S10); individual-level risks were taken from the out-of-fold predictions of a 10-fold cross-validation because in-bag predictions were strongly optimistic at this sample size, and the forest's out-of-bag C index was reported alongside.
