Shared Hub Genes and Immune Signatures of Sepsis and ARDS: A Multi-Omics Integration

Running head: Shared Hub Genes in Sepsis and ARDS

Article type: Original Research

---

ABSTRACT

Rationale: Sepsis is the most common trigger of acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS), yet shared molecular determinants remain undefined.

Objectives: To define the shared molecular intersection and blood immune microenvironment of sepsis and ARDS.

Methods: Two discovery cohorts were re-analyzed from raw matrices with off-target samples removed; differentially expressed genes intersecting co-expression modules were prioritized by machine learning and confirmed in two independent cohorts; and immune deconvolution, single-cell transcriptomics, Mendelian randomization, drug repurposing, and prognostic evaluation extended these.

Measurements and Main Results: Six shared hub genes (CDK5RAP2, CD177, GRB10, UPP1, EXOSC4, VNN1) discriminated disease from controls in external validation (areas under the curve 0.753 to 0.998) but not sepsis from ARDS. Both showed monocyte expansion with CD8 T-cell and natural killer cell depletion; complement activation was the only shared top-ranked pathway. Reverse Mendelian randomization constrained causal effects of ARDS on sepsis risk (minimum detectable odds ratio 1.04); the forward analysis was uninformative (minimum detectable odds ratio 2.26). In 479 septic shock patients, the six-gene risk score stratified mortality (log-rank P=0.002) yet added no increment beyond age, sex, and endotype (likelihood-ratio P=0.16), whereas a penalized 171-candidate signature retained prognostic information (optimism-corrected C index 0.622) with no hub gene. Docking nominated VNN1 as the preferred predicted binding partner.

Conclusions: Six hub genes mark a molecular intersection of sepsis and ARDS in a myeloid-expanded, cytotoxic-lymphocyte-depleted compartment; they are candidate disease-state markers rather than independent prognostic markers and require experimental validation.

Keywords: Transcriptome; Machine Learning; Mendelian Randomization Analysis; Single-Cell Analysis; Drug Repositioning

---

INTRODUCTION

Sepsis, life-threatening organ dysfunction from a dysregulated host response to infection (1), remains a leading cause of death in the critically ill and is the most common predisposing condition for the acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) (2, 3), acute hypoxemic respiratory failure with neutrophil-rich inflammation. Mortality in sepsis-associated ARDS remains high and no targeted molecular therapy exists (4). The two syndromes share much of their inflammatory machinery, including complement activation, cytokine release, and neutrophil and monocyte engagement (5), yet have been studied largely apart.

Shared-gene screens spanning the two diseases have been reported (6–8). The immunology of each condition is well characterized, from the biphasic septic response of early cytokine storm followed by protracted immunosuppression with lymphocyte apoptosis, myeloid-derived suppressor-cell expansion, and monocyte HLA-DR downregulation (9–12) to the neutrophil-rich, complement-activating lung inflammation of ARDS (2, 5). Individual molecules have drawn independent attention: the dynamic neutrophil CD177/CD10 ratio differentiates infection from non-infectious inflammation and stratifies mortality (13), CD177+ neutrophils are linked to necroptosis-driven inflammation (14) and stable subtype identity (15), and macrophage UPP1 drives a mitochondrial-ROS–cGAS–NLRP3 axis (16). Computational repurposing has also been attempted (17).

Three gaps follow from that work. Screen inputs were never re-curated: GSE76293 mixes disease samples with in vitro-cultured neutrophils and GSE69528 includes uninfected patients with type 2 diabetes, contaminants that confound differential expression and machine-learning inference alike. Validation was internal: candidates were nominated and tested in the same data, so the shared intersection has never faced discovery-strict external validation. Causal and cell-level questions were left open, and signature-matching repurposing inherits the reproducibility limits (18) and context-agnostic matching (19) of cancer-cell-line compendia.

We asked which genes and immune processes are coordinately dysregulated in both conditions and remain supported after re-curation, external validation, and orthogonal causal and single-cell testing. Differentially expressed genes intersected with disease-associated WGCNA (weighted gene co-expression network analysis) modules yielded 179 shared candidates, and machine learning with cross-disease testing confined to discovery data reduced them to six hub genes (*CDK5RAP2*, *CD177*, *GRB10*, *UPP1*, *EXOSC4*, *VNN1*), confirmed in two independent cohorts. We then tested whether shared genes surface consistently across cohorts, cell types, and analytic angles along four orthogonal lines: immune deconvolution and single-cell mapping with dual virtual knockout, pathway-level enrichment, Mendelian randomization with SMR-HEIDI, LD score regression, and colocalization, and drug repurposing quantified against nulls.

What emerges is a curated intersection: six hub genes that survive independent validation, a map of where they act within myeloid populations, genetic evidence bearing on their causal status, and a bounded set of candidate compounds. CD177 and UPP1 thereby extend from established sepsis markers into the shared sepsis–ARDS context.

METHODS

Two discovery cohorts were re-analyzed from raw probe-level matrices with off-target samples removed: GSE76293 (ARDS neutrophils; 12 cases, 12 controls; 70 in vitro-cultured samples excluded) and GSE69528 (sepsis whole blood; 83 cases, 28 controls; 27 uninfected patients with type 2 diabetes excluded) (Table 1). External validation used GSE100159 (sepsis; 33 cases, 12 controls) and GSE243066 (ARDS RNA-seq; 34 cases, 15 controls). Single-cell analysis used GSE151263 (PBMCs; 13,996 cells from three ARDS and four sepsis donors) and GSE167363 (CD14+ monocytes; 12,359 cells). Prognostic evaluation used GSE65682 (479 patients with septic shock, blood drawn at ICU admission; 28-day mortality, 114 deaths; Mars1-to-Mars4 endotypes) (20).

Differential expression (limma (21); |log2 fold change| > 1, adjusted P<0.05) intersected with disease-associated WGCNA (22) modules defined 179 shared candidate genes, followed by GO and KEGG enrichment (23). Eleven machine-learning classifiers (24) with SHAP interpretation (25) prioritized hub genes; ridge-regularized logistic models (26) assessed disease prediction; and single-gene and transferred-model performance was confirmed in the validation cohorts with meta-analysis. Prognostic models in GSE65682 comprised per-gene and joint six-gene Cox models (27), a LASSO-penalized Cox model (28) over the 171 platform-represented candidates, and a random survival forest (29), with bootstrap optimism correction and likelihood-ratio testing against a clinical model. Bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization (30) was complemented by power analysis, LD score regression (31), SMR-HEIDI (32), and colocalization (33). Immune composition was estimated by CIBERSORT (34) and deconvolution-independent ssGSEA (35), cell-cell communication by CellChat (36), single-cell analyses included donor-level verification, protein evidence came from the Human Protein Atlas Blood Atlas (accessed November 6, 2025), CMap repurposing was quantified against random gene-set nulls, docking was validated by redocking and MM/GBSA rescoring, and druggability was assessed from Open Targets (September 2026) and ChEMBL (release 37, accessed September 17, 2026).

Analyses used R 4.6.1, were performed from raw data, kept discovery within discovery cohorts, and applied two-sided tests with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment (37); because the validation cohorts also screened seven ARDS-nominated candidates down to six, their estimates carry a mild optimistic bias bounded by a seven-gene sensitivity analysis. Complete methods, parameters, and validity restrictions are provided in the Online Supplement (Supplementary Methods S1–S14).

RESULTS

Identification of Shared Candidate Genes

Differential expression analysis identified 557 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in ARDS and 1,458 in sepsis (Supplementary Table S6). WGCNA identified 13 disease-associated modules in ARDS and 11 in sepsis, with strong cross-cohort preservation (Zsummary 19.5 and 21.4). Intersection of DEGs with module genes yielded 179 shared candidates (Figure 1; Supplementary Table S7).

GO and KEGG Enrichment of Shared Candidate Genes

Among the 179 shared candidates, GO terms centered on immune activation and antigen presentation (positive regulation of cytokine production, MHC class II protein complex assembly), and KEGG pathways converged on Th1/Th2/Th17 differentiation, antigen processing and presentation, and NOD-like receptor signaling (Figure 2).

Machine Learning Identifies Six Hub Genes

Eleven machine-learning classifiers were trained on the 179 shared candidates in each discovery cohort (Figure 3A, B); AUCs of 1.00 in the small ARDS cohort (n=24) reflect overfitting and were not used for gene ranking, and label permutation excluded label-noise artifacts (Supplementary Figure S5). Selection required concordance across LASSO, random forest, and SHAP, stable but not unanimous (Supplementary Table S9). The consensus rule nominated nine ARDS and six sepsis candidates; cross-disease testing within discovery data retained six with same-direction discrimination in the opposite cohort (AUC 0.844 to 0.979), whereas *DYRK2* and *TCTN1* reversed direction (Figure 3C). External validation confirmed six of the seven ARDS-nominated candidates in both cohorts and refuted *MUC1* (AUC=0.575), leaving the final six (*CDK5RAP2*, *CD177*, *GRB10*, *UPP1*, *EXOSC4*, *VNN1*). The six span pyrimidine metabolism (*UPP1*), the RNA exosome (*EXOSC4*), pantetheinase signaling (*VNN1*), and the neutrophil-restricted marker *CD177*; these groupings are annotation-based, not demonstrated co-regulated modules (Figure 3D).

Expression Patterns and Enrichment of the Hub Genes

*UPP1* was upregulated in both diseases in all four cohorts, *CD177* most prominently in the ARDS cohorts, and *VNN1* and *EXOSC4* elevated with cohort-dependent effect sizes (Figure 4A–D). Single-gene GSEA showed shared inflammatory enrichment differing by tissue context: the sepsis discovery cohort enriched hub-gene networks for TNF and IL6/JAK/STAT3 signaling (strongest for *VNN1*), the ARDS neutrophil cohort showed negative enrichment for MYC targets and DNA repair, and both validation cohorts were dominated by interferon responses (for example, *CD177*: interferon-alpha response) (Figure 4E; Supplementary Figure S6).

Disease Prediction Model Construction

The six hub genes served as predictors in ridge-regularized logistic models visualized as nomograms (Figure 5). The ARDS model, trained on GSE76293 (n=24) and validated on GSE243066 (n=49), achieved a training AUC of 1.000 (an artifact of complete separation) and a validation AUC of 0.984; the sepsis model, trained on GSE69528 (n=111) and validated on GSE100159 (n=45), reached 0.993 in training but 0.708 in validation. Refitting with all seven nominated genes left the ARDS AUC unchanged (0.984) and slightly reduced the sepsis transfer (0.708 to 0.699), so screening did not inflate performance (Supplementary Table S11). An exploratory ComBat (38) adjustment restored the transfer AUC to 0.977, consistent with platform scaling as one contributor, though ComBat cannot separate platform from case-mix or severity effects (Supplementary Figure S7).

External Confirmation of the Six-Gene Signature

Single-gene AUCs, involving no model fitting, ranged from 0.753 to 0.998 in both validation cohorts (Figure 6; Table 2): *EXOSC4*, *VNN1*, *CD177*, and *GRB10* were high and consistent in both, *UPP1* discriminated more modestly in sepsis (0.753) than in ARDS (0.935), and *CDK5RAP2* performed worst in the ARDS cohort (0.782). Meta-analysis confirmed pooled discrimination for all six (Hedges g 1.14 to 2.32, all P<10⁻⁵), with heterogeneity concentrated in *CD177*, *UPP1*, and *VNN1* (I²=0.60 to 0.91) (Figure 6B; Supplementary Table S11). Dropping *CD177* left transferred ARDS discrimination intact (AUC 0.961 versus 0.984; Supplementary Table S11). In a disease-versus-disease contrast (three ARDS versus four sepsis donors), none of the six discriminated ARDS from sepsis (combined six-gene signature AUC=0.50; all exact P>0.4).

Prognostic Performance in Septic Shock

Prognostic association was tested in GSE65682 (479 patients with septic shock) (20). No hub gene was individually associated with 28-day mortality (hazard ratios per SD 0.92 to 1.16, all 95% confidence intervals spanning 1; nominal P=0.136 to 0.640; Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P=0.45 to 0.64; Supplementary Figure S20B); in the joint model, *UPP1* was the only significant term (hazard ratio per SD 0.64; 95% CI 0.44 to 0.95; P=0.026) and the model as a whole was not (likelihood-ratio P=0.067). The six-gene risk score nonetheless separated outcomes (log-rank P=0.002; Supplementary Figure S20A) but added no increment beyond age, sex, and Mars endotype (likelihood-ratio P=0.16; optimism-corrected C index 0.603 versus 0.606) (Supplementary Figures S20C to S20E). A LASSO Cox model over the 171 platform-represented shared candidates differed: the one-standard-error rule retained no gene, while the minimum-deviance penalty retained 15, none a hub gene (Supplementary Figures S21A, B). The penalized signature outperformed the six-gene score: optimism-corrected C index 0.622 alone and 0.638 with clinical covariates, 28-day AUC 0.698, and likelihood-ratio P=9.7×10⁻¹² against the clinical model (Supplementary Figures S21C to S21E).

Mendelian Randomization Under Limited Power

Inverse-variance-weighted MR did not detect a causal effect of sepsis on ARDS (β=−0.336; 95% CI −0.908 to 0.236; P=0.249), with consistent nulls across sensitivity analyses; reverse MR was likewise null (P=0.329) (Figure 7A, B). Post hoc power analysis revealed an asymmetry: the forward analysis had 28% power at a causal odds ratio of 1.5 (minimum detectable odds ratio 2.26), whereas the reverse was well powered (1.04) (Figure 7C; Supplementary Table S13). Only the reverse null therefore constrains causal effects; the forward null is uninformative rather than evidence of absence. Orthogonal checks confirmed a weak germline signal: LD score regression estimated near-zero genetic correlation (rg=−0.05; P=0.83) and SNP heritability h²≤0.0022 for both traits, and SMR-HEIDI for the five hub genes with cis-expression quantitative trait loci (*CDK5RAP2*, *CD177*, *GRB10*, *UPP1*, *VNN1*) showed no pleiotropy, no significant causal estimate, and no colocalization (Figure 7D).

Immune Microenvironment: Myeloid Expansion and Complement Activation

The six-gene signature score was higher in disease than in controls in both discovery cohorts (P=3.2×10⁻⁵ and 2.3×10⁻¹⁴) (Figure 8A). CIBERSORT deconvolution revealed a conserved myeloid-expansion and cytotoxic-lymphocyte-depletion phenotype shared by sepsis and ARDS: monocytes were expanded and CD8 T cells, CD4 memory-resting T cells, and resting natural killer cells depleted in all three interpretable cohorts, with M0 and neutrophil fractions additionally expanded in the two sepsis whole-blood cohorts; M0 fractions were read as myeloid enrichment rather than literal macrophage abundance (Figure 8B to F; Supplementary Table S2). Deconvolution-independent ssGSEA with 18 published lineage marker sets reproduced the same pattern (monocyte program P as low as 1.7×10⁻¹²). Regulatory T-cell direction diverged between the two methods. GSVA identified complement activation as the only shared pathway among the top-20 dysregulated immune processes of both diseases, but this uniqueness was threshold-dependent: relaxing the cutoff to 30 and 50 yielded five and seven shared pathways, all concordant in direction (Figure 8G, H; Supplementary Table S15).

Single-Cell Localization of the Hub Genes

Two single-cell cohorts covered complementary windows on the disease spectrum (Figure 9). In PBMCs (GSE151263), ARDS showed lower monocyte and natural-killer-cell fractions with B-cell and platelet expansion (Figure 9A, B). CellChat ranked MHC-I and MIF as the two strongest pathways in both states; MIF strength was higher in sepsis than in ARDS (1.24×10⁻³ versus 6.43×10⁻⁴; Figure 9B; Supplementary Figure S12). In CD14+ monocytes (GSE167363), MIF was again strongest in sepsis (3.16×10⁻³ versus 2.85×10⁻³ in controls; Figure 9F; Supplementary Table S25), and MHC-II led the healthy network (1.34×10⁻² versus 1.87×10⁻³). *UPP1* expression declined along the monocyte differentiation trajectory (four of five donors concordant; Figure 9C, G), sepsis monocytes occupied later pseudotime positions than controls, and metabolic programs peaked at sepsis and partially receded in ARDS, an inverted-U pattern read qualitatively. CellOracle (39) in silico knockout placed CEBPB as the dominant upstream regulator of *UPP1* in every stratum, with dependence rising from health to sepsis and receding in ARDS (Figure 9D, H); a per-donor analysis reproduced the effect in all five donors, and group-stratified scTenifoldKnk (40) showed perturbation spectra more concordant between the two disease states (rho 0.63 to 0.66) than between either and health (0.44 to 0.50) (Supplementary Figures S16 to S18). Donor-level pseudobulk DESeq2 (41) confirmed sepsis induction of *CD177* in classical monocytes (log2 fold change 6.68; P=0.002), the only hub-gene contrast significant at the donor level.

Protein-Level Validation and Therapeutic Relevance

In the Human Protein Atlas Blood Atlas, CD177 was upregulated in 9 of 10 infectious diseases versus controls (up to 13.6-fold), and VNN2 in all 10 (Figure 10A; Supplementary Figure S19). In the up-regulated CMap query, prespecified as hypothesis-generating and quantified against random nulls, the six-gene signature returned dexamethasone as the rank-one term (P=3.7×10⁻⁴); size-matched random signatures from non-hub candidates matched or exceeded this enrichment in 13 of 100, transcriptome-wide sets in none, and a stringency-matched background from genes up-regulated in both discovery cohorts in only 2 of 100 (Figure 10B; Supplementary Figure S3). The down-regulated query returned no glucocorticoid; an independent compendium replicated the up-regulated direction. Docking of 10 natural-product ligands, validated by redocking and MM/GBSA rescoring, nominated VNN1 as the preferred predicted partner, with luteolin strongest by Vina (−10.01 kcal/mol) (Figure 10C, D; Table 3; Supplementary Table S19); the two affinity measures favored VNN1 at the target level but disagreed on ligand ranking, so no ligand-level prioritization is supported (Supplementary Table S20). Open Targets and ChEMBL profiling showed *VNN1* and *UPP1* to be the only tractable targets, with no approved or clinical-stage small-molecule drug for any of the six.

DISCUSSION

This study yielded six hub genes shared by sepsis and ARDS (*CDK5RAP2*, *CD177*, *GRB10*, *UPP1*, *EXOSC4*, *VNN1*) against a shared background of complement activation and a myeloid-expanded, cytotoxic-lymphocyte-depleted blood phenotype. The six were nominated entirely within discovery data, confirmed as single-gene markers in two independent cohorts, and corroborated for CD177 protein across nine infectious contexts. Both readouts reproduced this phenotype. It is the blood counterpart of the immunosuppressive phase long described in sepsis, marked by lymphocyte apoptosis, myeloid-derived suppressor-cell expansion, and monocyte HLA-DR downregulation (9–12); the present analysis adds that it characterizes ARDS blood as well, where leukocyte expression separates reactive from uninflamed phenotypes (42).

The difference from earlier screens is threshold- and cohort-dependent, not a refutation: a screen with more permissive criteria nominated *CX3CR1*, *PID1*, and *PTGDS* as shared markers (6); in the curated data, all three are downregulated in sepsis blood, but none enters from the ARDS side: *PID1* falls short of the fold-change threshold (log2 fold change −0.79, adjusted P=0.006), and *CX3CR1* and *PTGDS* are not differentially expressed. *MUC1* was rejected at external validation and *DYRK2* and *TCTN1* reversed direction across cohorts. Validation cohorts narrowed seven candidates to six, so those estimates carry mild optimism.

The six genes have annotation-level links rather than arbitrary membership: *UPP1*, *VNN1*, and *CD177* map to the observed myeloid and neutrophil phenotype, macrophage UPP1 drives a mitochondrial-ROS–cGAS–NLRP3 axis experimentally in a lung adenocarcinoma model (16), and *CD177* is neutrophil-restricted and induced in infection (13–15); *EXOSC4*, *CDK5RAP2*, and *GRB10* link the set to RNA processing, centrosome function, and growth-factor signaling.

The two disease states are more similar to each other than either is to health: perturbation spectra correlated at rho 0.63 to 0.66 between ARDS and sepsis but only 0.44 to 0.50 with health, and both disease groups shifted monocytes to later pseudotime positions. These blood data fit ARDS as a partial extension of the sepsis state rather than a transcriptionally distinct entity: *UPP1* expression, CEBPB dependence, and metabolic activity peaked at sepsis and partially receded in ARDS, a cross-cohort pattern read qualitatively, consistent with the clinical transition from hyperinflammation to immune paralysis (10). Most of the sepsis transcriptome is shared across infection sources (43), and a recent multi-cohort analysis independently placed CEBPB in a severity-correlated necroptosis signature enriched in CD177+ neutrophils (14). The external-validation estimates concern discrimination from health, not between diseases; the combined signature did not discriminate them at the patient level (AUC=0.50; n=7 patients). Whether ARDS adds organ-specific immune regulation requires lung or bronchoalveolar sampling.

The Mendelian randomization result was null, with a clear asymmetry: the reverse null (minimum detectable odds ratio 1.04) does constrain causal effects of ARDS on sepsis risk, whereas the forward null is uninformative about small or moderate effects (28% power at an odds ratio of 1.5). That reading, with null SMR-HEIDI tests for the five genes with cis-eQTL, is compatible with the shared genes acting as reactive markers of the host response rather than causal drivers, the pattern expected of a neutrophil-activation signature such as *CD177*, whose ratio with CD10 tracks infection and stratifies mortality (13). Two caveats limit the inference: LDSC showed near-zero genetic correlation and SNP heritability (h²≤0.0022), so any modest true effect would have gone undetected, and the nulls cannot distinguish true absence of causality from phenotypic misclassification. Protein-level MR was precluded for five of the six genes (intracellular or membrane-bound). The six genes are therefore markers of disease state rather than established causal targets, whereas a genetically supported intermediate exists for this axis (44). All MR data sources and the eQTLGen reference were of European ancestry.

Individually, none of the six was associated with 28-day mortality in univariable models; jointly the risk score stratified survival (log-rank P=0.002) but added no increment beyond age, sex, and transcriptomic endotype (20) (likelihood-ratio P=0.16); part of that advantage reflects overlapping transcriptomic information. The prognostic information of the shared candidate set resided in 15 other genes, none a hub gene, and even there the one-standard-error rule retained nothing. A random survival forest added no gain (out-of-fold C index 0.610), so the advantage reflects variable selection, not nonlinearity. Diagnostic and prognostic information are largely separable in this gene set. Any prognostic development should start from the broader candidate set rather than the hub genes.

If a hub gene is carried toward experimental pharmacology, the choice should rest on druggability rather than binding-energy rankings. *VNN1* and *UPP1* are the only targets with pharmacological precedent: uridine analogs inhibit UPP1, including the co-crystallized benzylacyclouridine, and the nanomolar, orally active pantothenone RR6 inhibits VNN1, abolishing plasma vanin activity in rats (45) and reproducing *Vnn1* knockout protection in mouse acute kidney injury-induced acute lung injury (46). *CD177* is an antibody- or biomarker-level target, *GRB10* requires protein-protein interface targeting, and *CDK5RAP2* and *EXOSC4* are near-ubiquitous housekeeping proteins; the co-upregulated VNN2 paralog was not tested for selectivity. Vina and MM/GBSA disagreed on ligand ranking, so no ligand priority is supported. The CMap glucocorticoid signal matched only 2 of 100 stringency-matched disease signatures and returned no glucocorticoid in the down-regulated query, while its direction reflects the anti-inflammatory pharmacology of cancer-cell-line compendia of questioned reproducibility (18, 47) and context-agnostic matching (19). Both it and the docking energies are hypothesis-generating, consistent with endotype-dependent corticosteroid responses (48).

The prognostic analysis rests on a single cohort with internal validation. The discovery ARDS cohort is small (n=24), and all external validation contrasts disease with healthy controls only, so discrimination from non-infectious inflammation such as systemic inflammatory response syndrome or cardiogenic pulmonary edema is untested. The ARDS cohorts were not stratified by precipitating insult, so the study addresses sepsis and ARDS as diseases rather than sepsis-associated ARDS specifically. Both single-cell cohorts are blood-derived and comprise two to four donors per group; donor-level analyses reproduced nearly every direction, but only *CD177* induction is significant at that level. The sepsis prediction model generalized poorly (0.708 versus 0.984), with case-mix or severity differences plausible alongside platform scaling. The six-gene Cox model violated the proportional-hazards assumption globally (P=0.031) although no single gene did, so its joint estimate is time-averaged; the Kaplan-Meier split was insensitive to cut-point choice (log-rank P=0.00026 to 0.0019 across median, tertile, and quartile definitions); and ssGSEA immune comparisons retained Benjamini–Hochberg correction (Supplementary Table S24). No severity covariates were available, and corticosteroid exposure, which could shape the immune signatures and CMap result, is unknown here.

Clinically, the intersection is a shared host-response state, not a disease discriminator: the six genes cannot separate sepsis from ARDS (combined AUC=0.50) and could stratify trials enrolling both syndromes rather than differential diagnosis. Direct therapeutic targeting is unsupported by the causal-genetic evidence. The sepsis transfer decrement despite ComBat rescue shows that platform generalization, not discovery statistics, now limits such blood signatures.

CONCLUSION

Sepsis and ARDS share a definable molecular intersection: six co-expressed hub genes set against complement activation, myeloid-expanded and cytotoxic-lymphocyte-depleted blood, and monocyte metabolic reprogramming. Prespecified in discovery, confirmed externally and at the protein level, the intersection is bounded, measurable, and open to experimental test; tractability profiling nominates *VNN1* and *UPP1* for first testing, and whether either is a drug target requires experimental validation.
