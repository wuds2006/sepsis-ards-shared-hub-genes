Figure legends

**Figure 1. Study design and identification of 179 shared candidate genes. (A) Analysis flow. Two discovery cohorts were reanalysed from probe-level matrices; differential expression and disease-associated coexpression modules were intersected, and the shared candidates were carried through machine-learning prioritisation, external validation, immune deconvolution, single-cell mapping, Mendelian randomisation, drug repurposing and prognostic modelling. (B) Volcano plot of differentially expressed genes in the ARDS discovery cohort (GSE76293, neutrophils, 12 ARDS vs 12 control). (C) Volcano plot of differentially expressed genes in the sepsis discovery cohort (GSE69528, whole blood, 83 sepsis vs 28 control). In B and C, red points mark up-regulated genes and blue points down-regulated genes (|log2 fold change| > 1, Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05); grey points are not significant. (D) Module–trait correlation from weighted gene coexpression network analysis in the two discovery cohorts; bars give the correlation of each module eigengene with disease status and the trait-associated modules are labelled. (E) Four-set intersection of ARDS differentially expressed genes, ARDS disease-module genes, sepsis differentially expressed genes and sepsis disease-module genes; the central intersection contains the 179 shared candidates.**

**Figure 2. Machine-learning prioritisation and the shared immune signal. (A) Cross-validated area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of the twelve classifiers trained on the 179 shared candidates in the ARDS discovery cohort (GSE76293); eleven were estimable, and GBM, which could not be fitted at 19 training samples per fold, is marked with a dagger. Classifiers reaching complete separation carry no interval. (B) Mean absolute SHAP value of the twelve most important genes in the ARDS discovery cohort; hub genes among the twelve are shown in red. (C) Single-gene discrimination of the six hub genes in the two validation cohorts (GSE243066, ARDS; GSE100159, sepsis), with the combined six-gene signature shown for comparison; bars are the area under the curve with DeLong 95% confidence intervals. (D) M2 macrophage infiltration score in the two discovery cohorts, disease versus control. (E) Shift in immune-cell scores between disease and control samples in the two discovery cohorts; points give the log2 fold change, colour identifies the cohort and point size is proportional to −log10 adjusted P. P values in D and E are two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests.**

**Figure 3. Screening within the discovery data and the interaction network. (A) Cross-disease testing. Candidates nominated in one disease were scored in the other discovery cohort; points give the area under the curve, with the null value of 0.5 marked, and candidates retaining same-direction discrimination are distinguished from those that reversed direction and from those not retained. (B) Protein–protein interaction network of the shared candidates (STRING), with nodes sized by degree and edges drawn for high-confidence interactions; the six hub genes are highlighted.**

**Figure 4. Expression and single-gene enrichment of the six hub genes. (A–D) Transcript levels of CDK5RAP2, CD177, GRB10, UPP1, EXOSC4 and VNN1 in disease and control samples of the ARDS discovery (A, GSE76293), sepsis discovery (B, GSE69528), sepsis validation (C, GSE100159) and ARDS validation (D, GSE243066) cohorts. Boxes show the median and interquartile range, whiskers extend to 1.5 times the interquartile range and points are individual samples; P values are two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum tests. (E) Single-gene gene set enrichment analysis. Columns are the six genes, rows the Hallmark pathways, and each facet a cohort; colour encodes the normalised enrichment score, and asterisks mark Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05.**

**Figure 5. Disease prediction models built on the six hub genes. (A) Nomogram of the ridge-penalised logistic model for ARDS, fitted in GSE76293; per-gene points sum to a total score that maps to the predicted probability of ARDS. (B) Nomogram of the corresponding sepsis model, fitted in GSE69528. (C) Receiver operating characteristic curves of both models in their training and validation cohorts. (D) Distribution of model-predicted probabilities in control and disease samples of the validation cohorts (ARDS, GSE243066; sepsis, GSE100159); boxes show the median and interquartile range and points are individual samples. (E) Decision-curve analysis in the validation cohorts, showing net benefit across threshold probabilities for each model, with the treat-all strategy as a dashed line. Numbers in parentheses in C are areas under the curve.**

**Figure 6. External confirmation of the six-gene signature. (A) Single-gene area under the receiver operating characteristic curve with DeLong 95% confidence intervals in the ARDS validation cohort (GSE243066) and the sepsis validation cohort (GSE100159); the dashed line marks the null value of 0.5. (B) Standardised mean difference (Hedges g) of each hub gene between disease and control samples in the two validation cohorts. Points are per-cohort estimates with 95% confidence intervals, diamonds are fixed-effect pooled estimates, and the right-hand column gives the between-cohort heterogeneity (I²). Pooled g ranged from 1.14 to 2.32 (all P < 10⁻⁵), and heterogeneity was concentrated in CD177, UPP1 and VNN1 (I² = 0.60 to 0.91 for these three and 0.43 for GRB10). (C) Receiver operating characteristic curves of the six individual genes in the ARDS validation cohort; numbers in parentheses are areas under the curve. (D) The same curves in the sepsis validation cohort.**

**Figure 7. Shared immune microenvironment of sepsis and ARDS. (A) Six-gene signature score in disease and control samples of the two discovery cohorts (P = 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ and 2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴). (B) CIBERSORT cell-fraction shift between disease and control samples in the three interpretable cohorts. Each row is a cell type and each point the log2 fold change in estimated fraction (disease versus control); colour identifies the cohort and shape marks the significance of the two-sided Wilcoxon test, with point size proportional to −log10 adjusted P. Monocytes expanded and CD8 T cells, CD4 memory-resting T cells and resting natural killer cells contracted in direction across all three cohorts and significantly in both sepsis cohorts. The full 22-subtype composition of the four cohorts is shown as stacked bars in Supplementary Figure S24. (C, D) Gene set variation analysis of immune pathways in the ARDS (C) and sepsis (D) discovery cohorts. Bars show the top 20 pathways by significance, ordered by the disease-versus-control difference; complement activation is the only pathway shared by the top-20 lists of both diseases.**

**Figure 8. Single-cell localisation of the hub genes. (A) Uniform manifold approximation and projection of CD14+ monocytes (GSE167363, 12,359 cells), coloured by annotated subpopulation. (B) Subpopulation composition in healthy control and sepsis samples of the same cohort. (C) Monocle3 pseudotime of each subpopulation, healthy versus sepsis. (D) AUCell score of the six-gene signature by cell type in the peripheral blood mononuclear cell cohort (GSE151263, 13,996 cells from three ARDS and four sepsis donors); asterisks mark adjusted P < 0.05 between groups. (E) CellChat pathway strength in CD14+ monocytes, expressed as the log2 ratio of sepsis to healthy control for the fifteen pathways with the largest change; positive values indicate stronger signalling in sepsis. MHC-I and MIF were the strongest pathways in both states. (F) CellOracle in silico knockout of candidate upstream regulators, showing the simulated change in UPP1 (red) and EXOSC4 (blue) expression after knockout of each regulator.**

**Figure 9. Mendelian randomisation under limited statistical power. (A) Forward analysis (sepsis liability as exposure, ARDS as outcome, 18 instruments). Each row is one estimator; points are odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals, and the inverse-variance-weighted estimate was 0.71 (95% CI, 0.40 to 1.27; P = 0.249). (B) Reverse analysis (ARDS liability as exposure, sepsis as outcome, seven instruments); the estimate was likewise null (P = 0.329). (C) Post hoc power as a function of the causal odds ratio for the forward and reverse analyses; the dashed line marks 80% power. The forward analysis had 28% power at an odds ratio of 1.5 (minimum detectable odds ratio 2.26), whereas the reverse analysis was fully powered across the plotted range. (D) Summary-data-based Mendelian randomisation for the five hub genes with a cis-expression quantitative trait locus; points are the estimates at the top single-nucleotide polymorphism with 95% confidence intervals reconstructed from each effect size and its P value, and no gene reached significance or survived the HEIDI test and colocalisation.**

**Figure 10. Protein-level evidence and therapeutic relevance. (A) Human Protein Atlas Blood Atlas protein change of CD177 and VNN2 across ten infectious diseases relative to healthy controls; point size is proportional to −log10 adjusted P. CD177 was up-regulated in nine of ten diseases and VNN2 in all ten. (B) Connectivity Map results for the up-regulated six-gene query, with the top compounds from the CMap library and from an independent gene-expression compendium; bars are −log10 P and facets identify the source library. The top eight compounds were tied at P = 3.7 × 10⁻⁴; dexamethasone is one of them and is discussed because corticosteroids are the one drug class with trial evidence in both syndromes, and the order within the tie is arbitrary. (C) Site-specific docking of six hub-gene proteins against ten natural-product ligands (AutoDock Vina). Colour encodes predicted affinity, and the strongest cell is outlined. VNN1 was the preferred target and luteolin the strongest ligand (−10.01 kcal/mol). (D) AlphaFold confidence for the four targets without an experimental structure (CDK5RAP2, CD177, GRB10, EXOSC4; VNN1 and UPP1 were docked into the crystal structures 7SLV and 3EUF). Open circles give the mean pLDDT over the whole model and filled circles the mean over the residues lining the docking box; the dashed line marks pLDDT 70, the conventional boundary between confident and low-confidence regions. (E) Protein–ligand interaction profiler contacts of the VNN1–luteolin complex. Each row is a contact residue and the position along the axis is the contact distance; symbol and colour distinguish hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts and π-stacking. The same complex is shown as a two-dimensional interaction diagram and as a three-dimensional view of the pose in Supplementary Figure S25.**
