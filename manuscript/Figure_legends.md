# MAIN FIGURE LEGENDS (03_figures_RESULTS_NPG set)

Graphical Abstract. Unnumbered; see 05_supplementary_NPG/graphical_abstract/. Study overview from DEG-WGCNA intersection and machine-learning prioritization through immune profiling, single-cell virtual knockouts, and in silico drug prediction. No microRNA regulatory elements are depicted; microRNA regulation was not analyzed in this study.


**Figure 1. Convergent transcriptomic evidence nominates 179 shared candidate genes.**
(A) Volcano plot of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the ARDS discovery cohort (GSE76293, neutrophils, 12 ARDS vs 12 control). (B) Volcano plot of DEGs in the sepsis discovery cohort (GSE69528, whole blood, 83 sepsis vs 28 control). Red points indicate upregulated genes and blue points indicate downregulated genes (|log2 fold change| > 1, Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05). (C) Module–trait correlations from weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) in the ARDS discovery cohort; the turquoise module shows the strongest positive association with disease (r = 0.925). (D) Module–trait correlations in the sepsis discovery cohort; the blue module shows the strongest positive association with disease (r = 0.752). Bars are colored by direction of correlation (red, positive; blue, negative), with significance indicated by asterisks. (E) Four-set Venn diagram of ARDS DEGs, ARDS WGCNA module genes, sepsis DEGs, and sepsis WGCNA module genes; the central intersection of 179 genes defines the shared candidate set.

**Figure 2. Functional enrichment landscape of the 179 shared candidate genes.**
(A) Gene Ontology (GO) biological-process enrichment dotplot of the 179 shared candidate genes. Dot size indicates gene count per term and color indicates the Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P value. (B) Sankey–bubble diagram integrating GO biological process (BP), molecular function (MF), and cellular component (CC) terms; edges connect hub genes to their enriched terms, bubble size indicates gene count, and color indicates −log10(adjusted P). (C) KEGG pathway–pathway chord diagram of the 179 shared candidate genes, with ribbons linking co-enriched pathways; ribbon width is proportional to the number of shared genes between pathway pairs. Enrichment was computed with clusterProfiler against the full GO and KEGG annotation of the 179 genes.

**Figure 3. Machine-learning prioritization identifies six hub genes.**
(A) Cross-validated area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) of eleven machine-learning classifiers trained on the 179 shared candidate genes in the ARDS (red) and sepsis (blue) discovery cohorts. Error bars show the standard deviation of the cross-validated AUC; a permutation-derived lower bound of 0.975 is shown for classifiers reaching the AUC ceiling. (B) SHAP feature importance of the top ten genes by mean |SHAP value| in each discovery cohort. (C) Cross-disease candidacy assessment: single-gene AUCs of ARDS-nominated candidates tested in the sepsis discovery cohort (left) and of sepsis-nominated candidates tested in the ARDS discovery cohort (right). Confirmed hub genes (red) retain same-direction discrimination (AUC > 0.5); direction-reversed candidates (blue triangles) were excluded. (D) Protein–protein interaction network of the six hub genes, with nodes colored by functional category (pyrimidine metabolism, RNA exosome, pantetheinase/oxidative-stress signaling, other) and edges drawn at |r| > 0.7 among shared candidate genes.

**Figure 4. Expression patterns and single-gene gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of the six hub genes.**
(A–D) Transcript levels of the six hub genes in disease (red) and control (blue) groups across the ARDS discovery (A, GSE76293), ARDS validation (B, GSE243066), sepsis discovery (C, GSE69528), and sepsis validation (D, GSE100159) cohorts. Box plots show the median, interquartile range, and 1.5× interquartile range; P values were computed by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test (*P < 0.05; **P < 0.01; ***P < 0.001; ****P < 0.0001). (E) Single-gene GSEA normalized enrichment score (NES) heatmap for the six hub genes (rows) across eight Hallmark pathways (columns) in each of the four cohorts (panels). Red indicates positive enrichment and blue indicates negative enrichment; grey cells indicate pathway–gene combinations not estimable in that cohort. Asterisks indicate Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05.

**Figure 5. Disease prediction models built on the six hub genes.**
(A) Nomogram of the ARDS logistic regression model trained on GSE76293, in which per-gene point scores sum to a Total Score that maps to predicted ARDS probability. (B) Nomogram of the sepsis logistic regression model trained on GSE69528. (C) Receiver operating characteristic curve (left), calibration curve (middle), and decision curve analysis (right) for the ARDS model in the training cohort (red) and the independent validation cohort (blue). The calibration curve is shown with bootstrap resampling, and the decision curve displays net benefit across threshold probabilities. (D) Corresponding receiver operating characteristic, calibration, and decision curves for the sepsis model in training (red) and validation (blue) cohorts. AUC values are annotated in the legend of each receiver operating characteristic panel.

**Figure 6. External confirmation of the six-gene signature.**
(A) Single-gene area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) with DeLong 95% confidence intervals in the ARDS validation cohort (GSE243066, red) and the sepsis validation cohort (GSE100159, blue); the dashed line marks the null value of 0.5. (B) Meta-analysis of standardized effect sizes (Hedges g) for each hub gene across the two validation cohorts; squares indicate per-cohort estimates and diamonds indicate the fixed-effect pooled estimate with its 95% confidence interval. (C) Receiver operating characteristic curves of the six individual hub genes and the combined six-gene signature in the ARDS validation cohort. (D) Receiver operating characteristic curves of the six individual hub genes and the combined six-gene signature in the sepsis validation cohort. Numbers in parentheses in the legends are AUC values.

**Figure 7. Mendelian randomization detects no causal signal under limited statistical power.**
(A) Forest plot of the forward Mendelian randomization analysis (sepsis as exposure, ARDS as outcome, 18 instruments); each row shows the odds ratio and 95% confidence interval from inverse-variance-weighted (IVW), MR-Egger, weighted-median, and weighted-mode estimators, with the IVW estimate highlighted as a diamond. (B) Forest plot of the reverse Mendelian randomization analysis (ARDS as exposure, sepsis as outcome, seven instruments). (C) Statistical power as a function of the causal odds ratio for the forward (red) and reverse (blue) analyses; the dashed line marks 80% power. The forward analysis had 28% power to detect an odds ratio of 1.5, whereas the reverse analysis was fully powered across the tested range. (D) Summary-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) causal estimates for the five hub genes with available cis-expression quantitative trait loci, in sepsis (red) and ARDS (blue). Points indicate the SMR beta estimate at the top single-nucleotide polymorphism, with 95% confidence intervals; no estimate reached significance, and HEIDI tests rejected pleiotropy-driven signals for all gene–outcome pairs (all P_HEIDI > 0.05).

**Figure 8. Conserved myeloid expansion and complement activation across sepsis and ARDS.**
(A) Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) score of the six-hub-gene signature in disease (red) and control (blue) samples of the ARDS and sepsis discovery cohorts; P values were computed by two-sided Wilcoxon rank-sum test. (B–E) CIBERSORT-estimated immune-cell composition (stacked bars, 22 subtypes) in the ARDS discovery (B, purified polymorphonuclear cells, shown for transparency only), sepsis discovery (C, whole blood), ARDS validation (D, peripheral blood mononuclear cells), and sepsis validation (E, whole blood) cohorts. (F) Spearman correlation heatmap between hub-gene expression and CIBERSORT cell fractions across the four cohorts; red indicates positive and blue indicates negative correlation. (G, H) GSVA top-20 pathway heatmaps for the ARDS (G) and sepsis (H) discovery cohorts, with row z-scores of GSVA pathway activity; the complement activation pathway (arrow) is the only pathway shared by the top-20 dysregulated processes of both diseases, a uniqueness that is threshold-dependent (five and seven shared pathways at top-30 and top-50 cutoffs; Supplementary Table S15).

**Figure 9. Single-cell localization and regulatory architecture of the hub genes.**
(A–D) Peripheral blood mononuclear cell cohort (GSE151263; 13,996 cells from three ARDS and four sepsis donors). (A) Uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) of cells, colored by cell type. (B) CellChat differential heatmap of the number of inferred cell–cell interactions between the ARDS and sepsis groups; pathway-level analysis ranked MHC-I and MIF as the two strongest signaling pathways in both groups, with higher aggregate MIF strength in sepsis (1.24 × 10⁻³ versus 6.43 × 10⁻⁴; Supplementary Figure S12, Supplementary Table S25). (C) Monocle3 pseudotime trajectory along the monocyte differentiation axis (left), pseudotime distribution by disease group (middle), and AUCell score of the UPP1 co-expression program by disease group (right). (D) CellOracle in silico transcription-factor knockout, showing simulated expression change of UPP1 (red) and EXOSC4 (blue) upon knockout of each candidate upstream regulator. (E–H) CD14+ monocyte cohort (GSE167363; 12,359 cells). (E) UMAP of monocyte subpopulations, colored by cell type. (F) CellChat differential interaction heatmap between sepsis and healthy control. (G) Monocle3 pseudotime trajectory (left), pseudotime distribution by group (middle), and AUCell score of the UPP1 program by group (right). (H) CellOracle in silico knockout, showing simulated expression change of CDK5RAP2 (red), UPP1 (blue), EXOSC4 (teal), and VNN1 (dark blue) upon knockout of each candidate upstream regulator.

**Figure 10. Protein-level validation and therapeutic relevance.**
(A) Human Protein Atlas Blood Atlas protein log2 fold change of CD177 (red circles) and VNN2 (blue triangles) across ten infectious diseases versus healthy controls; point size is proportional to −log10(adjusted P). (B) Connectivity Map (CMap) up-regulated query results, showing the top glucocorticoid terms ranked by −log10(P); dexamethasone is the rank-one compound (P = 3.7 × 10−4), and prednisolone is the top-ranked term in an independent Drug Perturbations from Gene Expression Omnibus compendium (P = 4.4 × 10−5). (C) Site-specific molecular docking heatmap of six hub-gene protein targets (rows) against ten natural-product ligands (columns), with color indicating predicted binding affinity (AutoDock Vina, kcal/mol); VNN1 shows the strongest predicted affinity across the panel. (D) Three-dimensional binding pose of luteolin (green sticks) in the VNN1 co-crystallized site (blue cartoon), rendered by PyMOL; the predicted binding affinity is −10.01 kcal/mol. (E) Protein–ligand interaction profiler (PLIP) two-dimensional interaction diagram of the VNN1–luteolin complex, showing hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, and π-stacking interactions.

# ALTERNATIVE TEXT (Alt text) FOR ACCESSIBILITY

**Graphical Abstract.** A flow diagram showing the analysis pipeline of the study: two discovery cohorts yield 179 shared candidate genes by intersecting differential expression with co-expression modules; machine learning nominates six hub genes; the genes are then examined by immune deconvolution, single-cell virtual knockout, Mendelian randomization, and drug repurposing, converging on a shared molecular intersection of sepsis and ARDS.

**Figure 1.** Five panels showing convergent transcriptomic evidence: two volcano plots of differentially expressed genes in the ARDS and sepsis discovery cohorts; two module-trait correlation bar charts from weighted gene co-expression network analysis; and a four-set Venn diagram whose central intersection of 179 genes defines the shared candidate set.

**Figure 2.** Three panels showing functional enrichment of the 179 shared candidate genes: a Gene Ontology biological-process dotplot, a Sankey-bubble diagram linking hub genes to enriched terms across three ontologies, and a chord diagram of co-enriched KEGG pathways.

**Figure 3.** Four panels showing machine-learning prioritization: bar charts of cross-validated AUC for eleven classifiers in two cohorts; SHAP feature-importance plots of the top ten genes; scatter plots of cross-disease candidacy testing; and a protein-protein interaction network of the six hub genes colored by functional category.

**Figure 4.** Five panels showing hub-gene expression patterns: four box-plot panels comparing transcript levels of the six hub genes between disease and control groups in four cohorts, and a heatmap of single-gene gene set enrichment normalized enrichment scores across eight Hallmark pathways.

**Figure 5.** Four panels showing disease prediction models: two nomograms of ridge-regularized logistic models for ARDS and sepsis, each followed by receiver operating characteristic, calibration, and decision-curve analyses in training and independent validation cohorts.

**Figure 6.** Four panels confirming the six-gene signature externally: single-gene AUC forest plots with confidence intervals in two validation cohorts; a meta-analysis forest plot of standardized effect sizes; and receiver operating characteristic curves of individual genes and the combined signature in each cohort.

**Figure 7.** Four panels showing null Mendelian randomization results: forest plots of forward and reverse analyses with four estimators; a power curve showing 28 percent power forward versus full power reverse; and summary-based MR estimates for five hub genes, all non-significant.

**Figure 8.** Eight panels showing a conserved immune phenotype: six-gene signature scores by group; stacked-bar immune-cell compositions in four cohorts; a correlation heatmap between hub genes and cell fractions; and two GSVA top-20 pathway heatmaps in which complement activation, marked by an arrow, is the only pathway shared at the top-20 cutoff of both diseases.

**Figure 9.** Eight panels across two single-cell cohorts: cell-type UMAPs; a CellChat differential interaction heatmap between ARDS and sepsis groups; monocyte pseudotime trajectories with group distributions and UPP1 program scores; and CellOracle in silico transcription-factor knockout bar charts ranking upstream regulators of the hub genes.

**Figure 10.** Five panels on protein-level validation and therapeutic relevance: protein fold changes of CD177 and VNN2 across ten infectious diseases; Connectivity Map query results with dexamethasone ranked first; a docking heatmap of six targets against ten ligands; the three-dimensional binding pose of luteolin in VNN1; and a two-dimensional protein-ligand interaction diagram.
